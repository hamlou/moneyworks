## Chapter 1 — DAY ONE: YOUR HEALTH BILL (≈ 1:00–3:00)
⟨calendar slams: DAY 1 · scoreboard card: PROMISES 0 · LAWS PASSED 0⟩

So it is day one and President El-Sayed walks into the office and the first thing on the desk is the plan he talks about the most which is Medicare for All. [S5]

The idea is simple to say and very hard to do because the government becomes everybody's basic health insurance and the doctor bill goes to Washington instead of to you. [S5]

His campaign says you could still keep extra insurance from your job or your union but it should become redundant and unnecessary. [S5]

⟨price tag counter rolls⟩ Now the price. When Bernie Sanders had a plan like this in 2020 the Urban Institute said it would cost the federal government thirty four trillion dollars over ten years. [S8]

And a fellow at that same institute told reporters this year that with inflation it could now cost close to double that. [S8]

**Sixty something trillion. I checked my wallet. Not enough.** ⟨Dave opens wallet, one moth flies out⟩

So who pays? Here it gets honest because El-Sayed has said he would try to make the rich pay and that he hopes to avoid raising taxes on most families including people who earn around a hundred thousand dollars a year. [S9]

But his campaign did not say exactly how it would pay for it and a budget watchdog group called the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget said back in 2019 that taxes on the middle class would have to rise to pay for Medicare for All. [S9]

That same group also said that a lot of families could end up paying less in taxes than they pay today in premiums and copays. [S9]

⟨two-sided scale: TAXES ↑ vs PREMIUMS ↓, it wobbles⟩ So your paycheck might lose a piece on one side and win a bigger piece on the other side and nobody can tell you which one until the plan has real numbers. [S9]

Bridge Michigan checked it and their bottom line was that the claim it would raise taxes is likely correct but some experts think the total cost could go down for some families. [S9]

**So day one ends with one plan and zero laws. Hold that thought.**

## Chapter 2 — WEEK ONE: YOUR HOUSE (≈ 3:00–4:30)
⟨calendar: WEEK 1 · scoreboard: PROMISES 2 · LAWS 0⟩

In week one he goes after something every renter in America feels which is the rent. [S10]

He has vowed to ban large corporations from owning homes at all and to ban the computer programs that big landlords use to set rents together. [S10]

He also wants companies like Airbnb to pay a special fee for pushing people out of their neighborhoods. [S10]

⟨a giant suit-wearing BOX with a briefcase walks down a street buying houses, SOLD signs flip⟩ The picture in your head is a big company buying up the whole street but a housing expert at Michigan State said corporate buyers are only about two percent of the single family market. [S10]

**Two percent. That is one house on a street of fifty. Rude house though.**

And here is the funny part because a ban on big investors buying houses is not even only his idea. This year Congress passed a bipartisan housing bill that stops investors who already own three hundred and fifty homes from buying more. [S11]

So on this one he might find friends on both sides which in Washington is rarer than a cheap apartment. [S11]

## Chapter 3 — MONTH ONE: WHO FIGHTS BACK (≈ 4:30–6:30) — the villain = the money + the math
⟨calendar: MONTH 1 · scoreboard: PROMISES 3 · LAWS 0 · MONEY AGAINST: rolling⟩

Remember those thirty three million dollars from the start? **Cute.** [S4]

By the beginning of October the money spent in his Senate race had passed two hundred and five million dollars and that made it the most expensive race Michigan has ever seen. [S12]

⟨bar race: REPUBLICANS $78 M vs DEMOCRATS $63 M (as of Oct 10)⟩ Republicans had outspent Democrats seventy eight million to sixty three million by the middle of October. [S12]

And that same AIPAC super PAC from the primary is now reported to be sending up to nine million dollars to a group that backs his Republican opponent Mike Rogers. [S13]

**Have a guess how much of that money is on his side.** ⟨beat⟩ He gets help too. Bernie Sanders and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez each moved a million dollars to support him. [S12]

⟨Senate chamber, 100 seats lighting up⟩ But the biggest wall is not money it is math. A president cannot pass a law alone. In the Senate a bill needs a majority and most big bills need sixty votes to get past a filibuster. [S14]

⟨seats light up blue… stop far below 60⟩ So President El-Sayed would need sixty senators to say yes to Medicare for All and getting sixty senators to agree on something this big is about the hardest thing you can do in Washington. [S14]

**Laws passed in month one. Still zero.** ⟨scoreboard ticks: 0 → 0, sad ding⟩

## Chapter 4 — THE ATTACKS (≈ 6:30–7:45) — low point
⟨night, rain on the window, music drops⟩

And then there is everything that gets thrown at him personally.

The current president keeps calling him Mohammed and his opponent claimed he pledged support to the Muslim Brotherhood which a fact check found no evidence for. [S6] [S7]

He is also attacked for what he says about Gaza. He has said Israel's government committed genocide in Gaza and that he opposes unconditional military aid to Israel and he has also said Hamas committed war crimes. [S15]

His critics call those positions extreme and his supporters call them honest and you will hear both every single day of this presidency. [S15]

⟨Dave alone at the desk, lamp only⟩ So picture month two. The money is against him and the math is against him and half the news is against him.

## Chapter 5 — WHAT HE ACTUALLY DID BEFORE (≈ 7:45–9:00) — comeback
⟨flashback filter, DETROIT 2015⟩

So can this guy run anything? We actually have a test. In 2015 Detroit had just come out of bankruptcy and its health department had been handed to private companies and the mayor gave it to a thirty year old doctor to rebuild. [S16]

In his first year the department more than doubled in size. [S16]

After the Flint water crisis his team tested the water in every Detroit public school building which was ninety four buildings and they found nineteen schools with too much lead or copper. [S17]

**Ninety four buildings. I cannot even test my fridge.**

Now to be fair his campaign said he removed the lead from those schools and a news site found that the fixes were temporary and later tests still found lead so the honest version is that his team found the problem. [S18]

His department also gave more than five thousand pairs of free glasses to kids in Detroit schools. [S19]

Then in 2017 he left to run for governor of Michigan and in 2018 he lost that primary to Gretchen Whitmer. [S16]
