# SHORT 61 — What If Abdul El-Sayed Became President?

**Rebuild brief — v2, following PLAYBOOK_SHORTS.md and the newer STYLE_3D.md visual language.**  
Format: 1080×1920, 30 fps, target 50–65 seconds, Chatterbox voice.

## The story
A hypothetical presidency collides with a real political climate. The hook asks whether America would see a Muslim president as a president or as a threat. Then the Short makes the hypothetical explicit: El-Sayed is currently running for Michigan Senate, not president. It uses the reported attacks, his Medicare for All / corporate-money platform, and the constitutional obstacle of Congress. The ending resolves the question: winning the White House would not guarantee he could govern.

## Hook — frame zero
**VO:** “What if Abdul El-Sayed became president?”
**Frame 0:** His real licensed portrait already moving toward camera. Huge kinetic words: **WHAT IF HE WON?** Impact SFX from frame zero. No logo, no fade, no greeting.
Within the first six seconds: portrait punch-in → 3D White House doors swing open → 3D Dave turns toward camera in shock → “THE FIRST FIGHT” lands. The first question is emotionally clear with sound off.

## Shot plan
- **0–7s — The oath / first fight:** El-Sayed portrait and Oval Office / White House plate; 3D Dave enters a fully built 3D Oval Office environment and reacts. The hook is about acceptance, not a policy list.
- **7–18s — Reality check and attacks:** Senate-candidate context, then the Trump rally photo appears on the exact spoken name. Dave points at a floating news-screen UI, then rejects the accusation with a visible shrug / skeptical face.
- **18–31s — Healthcare and money:** hospital photo on “Medicare”; 3D hospital environment and a recreated healthcare UI panel. Insurance photo appears on “insurers”. Dave physically presents the policy card and reacts to the scale of the fight.
- **31–45s — The wall:** Capitol photo on “Congress”. A 3D bill prop moves toward the doors and gets blocked. Dave tries to push it through, stumbles back, then facepalms. This is an original visual gag, not a meme card.
- **45–60s — The actual question:** final 3D close-up / rooftop or Oval Office return. Dave looks directly at viewer. The final line asks whether he could win the country and convince Congress to let him govern.

## Visual language
- Use the actual 3D Dave character from src/v6/Dave3D.tsx / src/v6/kit.tsx; Dave is large and readable, never a tiny corner sticker.
- Build real 3D environments from geometry in Remotion: Oval Office, press / news room, healthcare room, Capitol hall, finale. Camera framing changes with the story.
- Pose and face swaps every 1.5–3 seconds at spoken-word anchors. Use point, present, panic, shrug, facepalm, shock and think. Give him purposeful physical actions; no random idle gestures.
- New world / hard scene change every 4–6 seconds. A meaningful visual change every 1–2 seconds in the opening and every 1.5–2.5 seconds thereafter.
- Real photos are licensed documentary plates inside designed UI frames, with Dave composited into the same scene. Each photo ID is used once and appears on the noun it illustrates.
- Use fast snap entrances, white pose-swap flashes, one-frame threshold / smear accents, punch-in camera changes, moving 3D props, and motivated SFX. Burst → hold; do not continuously wiggle everything.
- Follow the newer 3D style: no meme-format cards, no burned-in captions, no karaoke text, no polaroid/tape/sticker treatments, no old Damage Meter or generic branding bumper.
- Only short kinetic labels and important words. Keep Dave’s face and the real person’s face unobstructed.

## Factual guardrails
- As of October 27 2026, El-Sayed is a Democratic U.S. Senate candidate in Michigan. He is not a presidential candidate. The presidency is explicitly hypothetical.
- AP reported that Trump called him a “jihadist” and questioned his loyalty. El-Sayed rejected the accusations. Do not invent quotes or portray a hypothetical scene as real footage.
- His campaign platform includes Medicare for All and getting corporate money out of politics.
- Do not claim insurers funded attacks or paid opponents. Describe the possible policy conflict, not an unverified conspiracy.
- Congress must pass legislation; a president cannot enact Medicare for All alone.
